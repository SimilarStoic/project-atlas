direct creative-direction review of six founder-selected, approximately 30-second audiovisual excerpts. It establishes
creative direction and evidence handling only. It does not authorize media ingestion, provider calls, generation,
spend, production, publication or changes to the existing SimilarStoic visual system.

selected characteristic and quality authorities, not proof of commercial success and not copying templates.

## Reference interpretations

### `SS-CR-A-001` — explanatory depth

The founder valued long-form explanatory depth, approachable illustrated information, plausible 10+ minute content and
sponsor integration that occurs naturally within the topic rather than feeling arbitrarily inserted.

**Lesson:** SimilarStoic long-form should offer genuine explanatory value rather than motivational filler. A future
sponsorship may enter at a conceptually natural point only when clearly disclosed, editorially independent, tonally
appropriate and not allowed to distort the subject.

**Non-goals:** do not copy A's visual identity; A's narrator is not the SimilarStoic narrator target. Long-form audience
viability remains unproven.

### `SS-CR-B-001` — visual thought-density

The founder valued expressive synthetic narration relative to weaker AI voices, crude/simple illustrations, frequent
new visual ideas, rapid scene/image turnover and engagement maintained even when individual images barely move.

**Lesson:** high visual thought-density can matter more than expensive conventional animation. B's perceived image-idea
rate is a useful calibration reference, not a mandatory cut rate.

**Non-goals:** do not copy B's artwork or convert a future measured interval into a production threshold.

### `SS-CR-C-001` — audio-first explanation

The founder valued comparatively natural synthetic narration, simple drawings, rapid scene progression, narration that
stands on its own and visuals that help the viewer imagine and understand the spoken idea.

**Lesson:** audio carries the explanation; visuals provide a continuously changing interpretation layer. This
reinforces: **THE COMPLETE VIDEO MUST BE UNDERSTANDABLE FROM AUDIO ALONE.**

### `SS-CR-D-001` — recognisable crude charm

The founder valued real-human narration as a naturalness reference, crude/simple drawings, effective short-form
execution and a recognisable identity despite unsophisticated artwork.

**Lesson:** recognisability, personality and consistency can matter more than expensive polish. D supports the existing
SimilarStoic crude/charming direction.

**Non-goal:** do not copy D's exact visual language.

### `SS-CR-E-001` — scene mutation / scene development

The founder valued enthusiastic synthetic narration, fast rhythm, crude colourful artwork, rapid visual progression,
long-form viability and especially the progressive development of an existing composition.

**Lesson:** visual novelty does not require a wholly new generated scene every time. A useful base composition may
develop by adding/removing an object, changing expression or pose, adding a character or prop, circling/crossing out an
element, adding an arrow/label/timeline/comparison or progressively exaggerating a metaphor. This can improve perceived
activity, continuity and economics while preserving useful composition.

Scene mutation is a creative tool, not a requirement for every scene.

### `SS-CR-F-001` — primary narrator-performance benchmark

F is primarily an **audio and narrator-performance quality authority**, not a visual redesign target. The founder
valued naturalness, clarity, recognisable vocal identity, rhythm, pace, expression, phrase variation, forward momentum,
controlled enthusiasm, confidence, listenability, sustained energy and the absence of obvious robotic cadence.

**Narrator target:** F-level naturalness, clarity, rhythm and vocal-identity quality translated into a **distinct
SimilarStoic voice**. C additionally anchors realism/naturalness, B engagement/expression and E momentum/rhythm.

F is a perceptual benchmark only. Do not clone, impersonate or reproduce F's vocal identity, and never translate the
brief into “make it sound exactly like F.”

The founder recognises F's polished, expensive-looking visual production, but prefers the current SimilarStoic hamster,
artwork, backgrounds and hand-drawn world. Only limited visual characteristics transfer: cohesive art direction,
recurring identifiers, intentional finish, controlled progression, production coherence and sufficient quality not to
read as generic low-effort synthetic output.

## Composite SimilarStoic direction

> Current SimilarStoic visual world + F-level narrator-performance quality + B/C visual thought-density + E
> scene-development mechanics + D recognisable crude charm + A explanatory depth

The result must unmistakably remain **SimilarStoic**. Preserve the current hamster identity, Core visual authority,
approved acting poses, backgrounds, colour/art world, intentional imperfection, humour, visual-reference DNA,
reference-driven generation, environment authorities, composition grammar, break-frame logic, same-illustrator
coherence and visual QA.

The narrator explains; the hamster illustrates. The narrator carries the argument; the visuals continuously reinterpret
it. Useful visual beats include a new composition, scene mutation, reaction, prop reveal, annotation, metaphor,
diagram, evidence/source display, humour, comparison, timeline, hero/break frame and deliberate hold. Ask how many
useful distinctive visual ideas support the current thought, not how elaborately one image can be animated.

## Observation is not a rule

Approximate refresh frequency, scene/mutation density, pitch movement, pacing, pause frequency or beat duration may be
retained later as descriptive evidence only when grounded to a verified private clip. They are not universal production
thresholds. Meaning, narrative function, clarity, founder judgement and later audience evidence govern adaptive timing.
Conveyor should learn **attention rhythm**, not cargo-cult timings or “more cuts.”

## Governance layers

1. **SimilarStoic invariants** resist casual drift: brand, hamster identity, approved references, art/background
   direction, editorial/factual integrity, audio-first comprehension, personality and same-illustrator coherence.
2. **Creative-reference lessons** are evidence-backed starting points: F performance quality, B/C thought-density,
   E scene mutation, D crude charm and A explanatory depth.
3. **Adaptive production choices** may vary with the script, context, controlled comparison, founder judgement and
   later audience evidence: timing, beat count, annotation, mutation/composition balance, intensity, humour, music,
   transitions and holds.

References are selected characteristic authorities, not templates. Provisional repeated preferences may become
revisable defaults; none should silently become an immutable mechanical rule.

## Future exact anchors

Once a verified private excerpt exists, a separately reviewed evidence record may identify exact intervals for narrator
cadence/naturalness, scene mutation, rapid visual turnover, audio-first explanation, sustained rhythm or recognisable
identity. Do not invent anchors. Prefer “compare against `SS-CR-F-001` at approved interval X–Y” over imitation language.
