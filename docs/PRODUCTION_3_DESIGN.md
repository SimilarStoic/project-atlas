## Editorial design

### Core idea

A £300 monthly upgrade maintained for twenty years totals £72,000 in cash payments. That does not make the choice bad;
it makes the recurring commitment large enough to deserve a deliberate long-term decision.

### Audience value

Young UK professionals can treat a pay rise as permission for several permanent upgrades without seeing how much of
their new flexibility becomes fixed monthly spending. The piece offers a neutral test for intentional spending rather
than guilt about small pleasures.

### Evidence and claim boundary

The exact £72,000 figure is deterministic arithmetic: `£300 × 12 months × 20 years`. It is a nominal cash total and
must not be described as an investment outcome, guaranteed saving, present value, inflation-adjusted figure or cost of
debt. Examples such as a nicer flat, car or routine are illustrations, not claims about what any viewer should cut.

MoneyHelper's UK Budget Planner supports comparing income with outgoings and reviewing spending categories. Its current
household-budget guidance supports listing regular expenses and distinguishing essential from non-essential spending.
Execution should freeze and recheck those pages before creating Claims:

- [MoneyHelper — Budget planner](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/everyday-money/budgeting/budget-planner)
- [MoneyHelper — Create a household budget](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/blog/everyday-money/create-a-household-budget-for-your-family)
- [GOV.UK — Managing money, savings and debt](https://www.gov.uk/cost-of-living/managing-money)

Do not imply that all lifestyle improvement is wasteful, that £300 is affordable or unaffordable for a particular
viewer, that cutting it produces £72,000 of wealth, or that a pay rise should automatically be saved or invested.

### Target

- **Duration:** approximately 45–52 seconds
- **Format:** 1080×1920 vertical short
- **Audio-only comprehension:** mandatory
- **CTA:** none; the payoff is the decision question

## Exact Production #3 Script

> That £300-a-month upgrade is not a £300 decision.
>
> Keep it for twenty years and the payments add up to £72,000.
>
> No investment return. No scary forecast. Just £300, twelve times a year, for twenty years.
>
> This is how lifestyle inflation hides. A pay rise arrives, the nicer flat, car or routine becomes normal, and the
> extra income quietly turns into a permanent outgoing.
>
> That doesn’t make the upgrade bad. Money is for living. But recurring choices deserve a bigger question than,
> “Can I afford it this month?”
>
> Ask: would I still choose this if I saw the twenty-year total first?
>
> Keep the upgrades you value. Pause the ones you barely notice.
>
> The expensive part isn’t enjoying more. It’s letting “temporary” become automatic.

The Script is 126 words under the repository's normal word-token convention. Every factual element is audible. The
headline arithmetic should be independently recalculated during research and final editorial QA.

## Six-beat visual plan

| Scene | Narration beat | Hamster performance and visual idea | Reuse / bespoke plan | Motion purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | “£300-a-month upgrade” | Hamster happily accepts one small `£300 / month` tag; a long folded receipt begins to open behind it. | Reuse calm indoor language; likely new receipt-holding reaction pose only if existing poses fail. | One quick receipt reveal establishes the hidden scale. |
| 2 | “twenty years … £72,000” | Twelve simple month tokens stack into a year, then twenty year blocks form a long path ending at `£72,000`. | Reuse abstract reach-boundary language and locally drawn tokens. | Sequential grouping makes the multiplication legible without dashboard polish. |
| 3 | “No investment return” | The hamster wipes away decorative forecast arrows, leaving only `300 × 12 × 20`. | Reuse things-in-hands pose where natural; local chalk/mark treatment. | Removal focuses attention on plain arithmetic. |
| 4 | “pay rise arrives” | A larger pay packet flows into three recurring pipes: home, car and routine; the hamster notices the flow becoming fixed. | Sparse bespoke local props; environment generation only if deterministic treatment cannot meet Same-illustrator QA. | The flow settles to show temporary income becoming recurring outgoings. |
| 5 | “bigger question” | Hamster compares a tiny monthly tag with the unfolded twenty-year receipt. | Reuse sorting/decision pose or things-in-hands pose. | A deliberate comparison hold supports the spoken question. |
| 6 | “keep … pause” | Hamster keeps one valued object and calmly pauses two automatic conveyor tokens. | Reuse sorting pose and indoor tabletop composition. | Two small stop/release beats end with agency rather than deprivation. |

Numbers may appear as minimal factual props because narration states them fully. Typography remains sparse,
mobile-readable and subordinate to the hamster. No approved gross-up break-frame is needed; the story benefits from a
quiet accumulating reveal rather than emergency overreaction.

## Asset and assembly plan

Start with a zero-provider feasibility assembly using the approved sorting and things-in-hands poses, indoor example,
abstract example, composition grammar and locally drawn receipt/tokens/pipes. Reuse only when the action reads
naturally. If scene 1 lacks emotional clarity, one isolated receipt-holding pose is the highest-value generation gap.
If scene 4 cannot meet Same-illustrator QA locally, one sparse environment call may be proposed after the pose.

Keep the Production #2 assembly method: isolated character, separate environment, deterministic composition,
character-first hierarchy, warm off-white negative space, restrained flat colour and purposeful foreground overlap.
Do not generate a full scene, distort an existing pose, build a rig or add generic cinematic treatment.
