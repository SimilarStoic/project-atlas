## Editorial design

- **Opportunity:** `ai-workflow`
- **Title:** **Three AI workflows that make a junior analyst more valuable**
- **Audience value:** practical use of generative AI for junior analysts without promising a career outcome.
- **Core method:** widen the question; delegate the dull first pass; turn human review into a repeatable checklist.
- **Safety boundary:** consequential output requires human verification and sensitive information does not belong in an
  unapproved tool.
- **Sources:** the UK Government AI Playbook, guidance to civil servants on generative AI, the Data and AI Ethics
  Framework, and the OECD's 2025 review of generative AI and productivity.

The content remains UK-relevant and avoids claims that AI guarantees promotion, productivity or employability. The
hamster illustrates the method while the narration remains complete without the visuals.

## Final script

> Using AI faster will not make you a valuable junior analyst. Using it with better judgement might.
>
> Three workflows help.
>
> First: widen the question. Ask AI for missing assumptions, counterarguments and sources. Then verify anything
> important yourself.
>
> Second: give it the dull first pass. Clean notes. Compare tables. Draft the structure. You decide what matters, what
> looks wrong and what reaches another person.
>
> Third: turn your review into a checklist. Evidence. Numbers. Privacy. Tone. Run the same checks every time.
>
> AI can help with research, summaries and routine drafting. It can also produce confident mistakes, and sensitive
> information does not belong in an unapproved tool.
>
> Speed is useful. Speed without checking creates mistakes faster.
>
> The junior who finds gaps, verifies claims and improves the process becomes more useful than the one who just
> produces more.
>
> Use AI for momentum. Keep judgement as your signature.
